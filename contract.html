Changes and additions should be reduced to a written change order (text or email acceptable) signed or confirmed by both the Customer and the Contractor, stating the effect of the changed work on the contract price and completion date. A written change order will only be on a form or format prepared by the Contractor.

(b) The Customer may elect to orally authorize a change order, in which case the Contractor shall proceed as directed by the Customer or the Customer's authorized representative. Unless otherwise agreed by the Contractor, change orders shall be paid in full, up front, before the changed work is performed.

(c) Unless a change order has been prepared in writing and signed by both parties, the cost to the Customer of any changed or additional work, or work ordered on a time and materials basis, shall be calculated as follows:

  1. Administrative / supervisory time (including research and preparation of change order requests, whether or not the work is ultimately performed): $225.00 per hour.

  3. Excavator with FAE mulching attachment: $375.00 per hour.

  5. Machine Operator / Excavator: $155.00 per hour.

  10. Standby / delay charge for Customer-caused delay (including failure to provide access, incomplete marking or locates, or Customer-supplied subcontractor delay), per Section 6: $10,000.00 per day.
  11. Applicable sales and use taxes.
  12. If any completion date has been promised, the date will be equitably adjusted for changed and additional work.

6. Scheduling; Weather and Fire Restrictions

Contractor will expeditiously complete the Work, subject to availability of equipment, materials, and the site. Start and completion dates are good-faith estimates. It is the responsibility of the Customer to permit Contractor access to the site and to forward timely and complete directions so the Work can proceed as planned. Mastication work is subject to conditions beyond Contractor's control, including weather, ground conditions, equipment breakdown, geotechnical conditions (including failing slopes or hillside movement), additional work required by county, state, or federal jurisdictions, additional engineering requirements, and fire-season restrictions — including DNR Industrial Fire Precaution Levels (IFPL), burn bans, hoot-owl restrictions, and red-flag warnings — which may prohibit or limit machine operation. Delays from such causes extend the schedule and are not a breach of this Agreement. Delays caused by the Customer, and winter conditions work, will be billed according to Section 5(c). Contractor will communicate schedule changes promptly.

7. Customer Responsibilities

Before Work begins, Customer shall, at Customer's sole responsibility and expense:

(a) Utility Locates. Call 811 to have public utilities located and marked, and physically mark all PRIVATE underground installations, including septic tanks, drainfields, irrigation lines, water lines, power to outbuildings, propane lines, and wells. Contractor is not responsible for damage to unmarked or inaccurately marked underground installations.

(b) Boundaries. Clearly mark all property lines and the boundaries of the work area. Customer warrants the work area is within Customer's property or legal authority. Contractor is not responsible for surveying property lines. Customer is solely responsible for any encroachment, including timber trespass claims, arising from incorrect boundary information.

(c) Trees and Features to Preserve. Clearly and physically mark (flagging tape or paint) all trees, vegetation, and features to be preserved. Anything within the work area not marked for preservation may be masticated.

(d) Access. Provide equipment access to the work area adequate for tracked machines and support vehicles, and remove vehicles, equipment, livestock, and personal property from the work area and access route.

(e) Permits. Obtain and pay for any required permits or approvals (county clearing / critical areas permits, HOA approvals, Forest Practices approvals, agency authorizations, etc.), unless this Agreement expressly assigns permitting to Contractor. Contractor may rely on Customer's representation that all required approvals have been obtained. All charges for surveying, permitting, or related services shall be in addition to a Fixed Price and paid by the Customer.

8. Nature of Mastication Work; Site Conditions; Concealed Conditions

(a) Customer acknowledges mastication is heavy mechanized work. Normal and expected results include: mulch and wood debris left on the ground; ground disturbance and track marks; ruts in soft soils; minor damage to residual trees within or at the edge of the work area; and dust and noise during operations.

(b) Flying Debris. Mastication throws debris. Customer shall keep all persons, pets, and livestock at least 300 feet from the machine during operation and shall so advise household members, employees, guests, and neighbors.

(c) Concealed and Unknown Conditions. Contractor will familiarize itself with the project and premises but will not disturb existing improvements or soil in order to further inspect, and cannot ascertain unobserved or unknown conditions, including soil conditions, underground springs, buried debris, rock outcroppings, or metal. The scope of Contractor's Work does not include such unknown or unobserved conditions. If Contractor encounters concealed conditions materially increasing the cost or risk of the Work (rock outcroppings, metal, debris piles, stumps, springs or saturated ground, hazardous materials), Contractor will notify Customer, and the changed or additional work will be priced under Section 5. Contractor may cease affected Work until agreement is reached.

(d) Agency-Required Work. If the county fire authority, the Washington Department of Natural Resources, or any other government agency subsequently requires work to be performed by Contractor which is not stated in Section 3, or if Contractor discovers defects or problems in the parcel or soil which should be corrected to conform to safety requirements or standard practices, Contractor will advise the Customer of the required changes and proceed to perform such changes pursuant to Section 5 of this Agreement.

9. Insurance; Limitation of Liability

(a) Contractor maintains general liability insurance and is registered as a contractor with the State of Washington. Certificates of insurance are available on request.

(b) To the maximum extent permitted by law, Contractor's total liability for any claim arising out of this Agreement or the Work is limited to the Contract Price. Neither party is liable for consequential, incidental, or special damages, including lost profits, lost timber value, or diminution in property value.

(c) No Warranty of Regrowth Control. Mastication controls existing vegetation; Contractor makes no warranty regarding regrowth, invasive species, or future vegetation conditions.

(d) Nothing in this section limits liability that cannot be limited under applicable law.

11. Cancellation

(a) By Customer. Customer may cancel more than seven (7) days before the scheduled start date and receive a refund of the deposit less any costs actually incurred. If Customer cancels within seven (7) days of the scheduled start, or after mobilization, Contractor may retain from the deposit a mobilization / rescheduling charge of $____________________ plus costs incurred, refunding the remainder.

(b) By Contractor. If site conditions, safety, legal restrictions, or Customer's failure to perform its responsibilities under Section 7 prevent the Work, Contractor may cancel or reschedule; any unearned deposit will be refunded.

12. Termination

If this Agreement is terminated by either party for any reason, the Contractor will be paid the price of the Work performed through the date of termination, including mobilization, standby, and demobilization costs, calculated under Section 5 for Cost-Plus work or by percentage of completion for Fixed Price work. This termination provision is a liquidated damages provision intended to fully compensate the parties for all claims related to a partially completed project, including all consequential and incidental damages related to the Contractor's performance of the Work, and applies to all terminations whether for cause or otherwise.

13. Default; Stop Work; Lien Rights

If Customer fails to pay amounts due, Contractor may stop work and pursue all remedies available at law, including recording a lien against the property under chapter 60.04 RCW (mechanics' and materialmen's liens) for private projects, or asserting claims against the retainage and payment bond under chapters 60.28 and 39.08 RCW for public works projects, or under the federal Miller Act (40 U.S.C. § 3131 et seq.) for federal projects, as applicable. For residential and applicable commercial projects, Customer acknowledges receipt of the Notice to Customer disclosure statement attached as Exhibit A and required by RCW 18.27.114.

14. Disputes and Remedies; Governing Law

If a dispute arises between the parties, the parties shall promptly meet and attempt in good faith to resolve the dispute. If the dispute cannot be resolved, Contractor may stop work and pursue its lien and bond rights under Section 13 and all other remedies at law. This Agreement is governed by Washington law, except that federal law governs where the Work is performed under a federal contract. Venue for any action lies in the superior or district court of the county where the job site is located, unless a government prime contract requires a different forum. The prevailing party in any action to enforce this Agreement is entitled to recover its reasonable attorneys' fees and costs.

15. Entire Agreement; Miscellaneous

This Agreement, including Exhibit A, any incorporated government contract provisions under Section 16, and any written change orders, is the entire agreement between the parties and supersedes all prior discussions. If any provision is held unenforceable, the remainder stays in effect. This Agreement may be signed in counterparts, and electronic or scanned signatures are effective.

16. Government and Public Works Projects

The following apply only if a State / Municipal or Federal box is checked in Section 2:

(a) Order of Precedence. Where Contractor performs as a subcontractor under a government prime contract, the mandatory flow-down provisions of the prime contract (including applicable FAR / agency clauses on federal projects) are incorporated by reference and control over any conflicting provision of this Agreement, but only to the extent required by law.

(b) Prevailing Wages. On Washington public works, Contractor will pay prevailing wages and file Intents and Affidavits as required by chapter 39.12 RCW. On federal projects, Davis-Bacon Act wage determinations apply where required.

(c) Retainage and Bonds. Public works retainage and payment / performance bonding, where required, are governed by chapters 60.28 and 39.08 RCW (state) or the Miller Act (federal), and Contractor reserves all claim rights thereunder.

(d) Fire and Land-Management Requirements. Contractor will comply with agency-issued fire plans, IFPL shutdowns, spark-arrestor / water-supply requirements, and inspection holds. Agency-directed suspensions extend the schedule and, where caused by conditions not the fault of Contractor, standby will be billed under Section 5(c) unless the governing prime contract provides otherwise.

(e) Certifications. Contractor will provide certified payroll, insurance certificates, and other documentation reasonably required by the contracting agency.
